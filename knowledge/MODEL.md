# The model, in plain words

Source: Seth Godin, "Building a second-wave AI business" (6 October 2026), https://seths.blog/2026/10/but-is-it-a-business/ . Paraphrased in our own words. The scoring and coaching flow are Strategy Sprints'. Read the original.

## 1. Savings is a race nobody wins
Most AI wins so far cut cost or add speed. That's an easy sale (free sample, instant benefit) but a bad business: a competitor can always promise more savings, and once the savings are banked there's nothing left to sell. The opportunity is generative: AI that creates growth, connection or new capability.

## 2. Sell to people who prefer to pay more
Price-sensitive buyers make growth hard. Buyers with expensive problems and real budgets don't just tolerate a premium price, they prefer it: it signals you're serious. Look for a real problem, an existing budget, and something they can't get elsewhere.

## 3. What people really pay for (3 layers)
- **Root (people):** status (I matter), belonging (people like me accept me), freedom from fear (I'm safe). Fear comes first: you can't chase status in survival mode. Many premium purchases are fear relief dressed up as something else.
- **Middle:** legitimacy, transformation, a story to belong to, control, certainty, protection, trust, health, leverage.
- **Outer (easy to measure):** access, capital, time, attention, convenience, efficiency, delight, experiences, beauty.
A business worth building delivers the middle, justified by the outer. Nobody shops for transformation.

## 4. What businesses really pay for
The buyer spends someone else's money, so the question under every purchase is "what will I tell my boss?"
- **Root:** avoid blame (nobody got fired for buying the leader), claim credit (I brought in something that worked), reduce uncertainty (we can plan, the chaos drops).
- **Middle:** growth, efficiency, compliance, advantage, talent, morale, resilience, optionality, speed, legitimacy, relationships.
- **Outer:** cost and time savings, data, access, convenience, integration, reporting, support.
Mechanics without a story is the race to the bottom.

## 5. New vs repeat, commodity vs chaos
Repeat purchases are won on switching costs, relationships and steady improvement: you replace someone, so be cheaper, easier, or tell a better story. New purchases need an internal champion with a story that serves their career. Commodity buyers fear paying too much; buyers in chaotic, interesting spaces fear choosing wrong.

## 6. The forcing function
Without a crisis, even a perfect offer sits in the pipeline: committees, stalled pilots, champions reassigned. Three crises force a decision: a competitor moved; the technology changed (AI is both the crisis and the answer right now); the market shifted (regulation, cost collapse, culture). Sell into a crisis that already exists, so the champion can say "I found it".

## 7. What is not a moat
- Naked AI (a gateway to a frontier model): token costs eat the margin, nothing to defend.
- Hidden prompts: real value, no protection.
- Selling one at a time: brutally expensive.

## 8. A theory of profit (what becomes a business)
1. Builds its own data stack: small, specific, trusted, owned with the users, working for them, not trapping them.
2. Has a built-in network: users see each other, or have an obvious reason to spread it.
3. Solves an expensive problem for people with resources, beyond time and money (reassurance, capability, connection), priced to match.
4. Is bootstrappable: conceptual, not infrastructure.
Plus asymmetric information: help a group pool what it knows against a party that hoards it.

## 9. Data reality
Users fill a data stack only if it's effortless AND feels safe, and those two pull against each other. The best stacks use data people already create and are already comfortable with, and give it back to them in a better form. Always answer: who controls this, and what if it goes wrong?

## 10. Two extra models
- **Sponsored:** a foundation, brand, bank or fund with aligned interests pays, the users get it free. One relationship replaces thousands of sales. Works when the group is large and underserved, the value is visible to the sponsor, and the data serves both.
- **Cheap inference:** structure, pattern, aggregation and organisation at volume (forms, documents, transcription, record matching). Unglamorous, huge, underserved. Margins improve as models get cheaper.

## How we score it (Strategy Sprints)
7 questions, 0-2 each, max 14: creates value, worth paying for, root drive, champion story, forcing function, own data, network. 12-14 WORTH PAYING FOR, 9-11 PREMIUM-READY, 6-8 STUCK IN THE MIDDLE, 0-5 RACE TO THE BOTTOM. Flags: COST-CUT TRAP (Q1=0), NO FORCING FUNCTION (Q5=0), NO MOAT (Q6=0 and Q7=0); any flag caps the verdict at PREMIUM-READY.
